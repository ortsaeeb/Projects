How to scan

  1. Pick a store above and walk its clearance endcaps, garden center, seasonal aisle and overhead racks for items on these lists.
  2. In the Home Depot app with that store selected, an item that shows out of stock or not available while it's sitting on the shelf is the strongest penny sign.
  3. Scan the product's own UPC barcode at self-checkout. Don't scan the yellow clearance sticker. That can call an associate over.
  4. $0.01: buy it. Any other price: decide if the clearance price is still a good flip, otherwise leave it.
  5. If an associate says penny items can't be sold, accept it politely. Stores can refuse, and staying welcome matters more than one item.
  6. Record each scan with Log scan. Over a few weeks you'll learn which stores penny and which pull items early.
  7. Before you list anything for sale, look it up on cpsc.gov/Recalls. Selling a recalled product is illegal.

What the store statuses mean

Clearance: the store's system has the item in clearance. With no stock showing, the app says it's unavailable, which is the usual penny pattern.
Inactive: not a regular item at this store anymore. If stock still shows, scan it.
Deleted: removed from this store's system. Usually already pulled, but leftovers do turn up.
Active: normal stock at normal price. Left off these lists.

These meanings come from how the statuses behave, not from Home Depot. A penny price never shows online, so every item here has to be confirmed by scanning it in the store.
